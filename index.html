Research

My research integrates electrochemical experimentation, functional materials development, and multiphysics modeling to understand and optimize energy conversion systems. By combining computational simulations with experimental validation, I aim to develop efficient and sustainable technologies for hydrogen production, electrochemical energy conversion, and carbon utilization.

My doctoral research investigates transport phenomena and reaction mechanisms in electrochemical reactors through coupled experimental and computational approaches. These studies span metal–CO₂ electrochemical systems, multiphase transport processes, and the development of reactor designs for enhanced performance and efficiency.

⚗️

Metal-CO2 Electrochemical Systems

Modeling Mg-CO2 and Zn-CO2 primary batteries for simultaneous H₂ production, power generation, and CO2 utilization via multiphysics transient models.

🌊

Multiphase Flow & Bubble Transport

Euler–Euler bubble flow dynamics in electrolyzers and metal-air batteries; studying gas-phase distribution and its effect on electrochemical performance.

💻

Multiphysics Modeling & Simulation

3D/2D transient COMSOL models integrating dilute solution theory, porous electrode theory, Darcy's Law, and electrochemical kinetics validated against experimental data.

♻️

CCUS Technologies

Developing scalable CO2 capture, conversion, and utilization technologies addressing critical challenges in carbon neutrality and renewable energy integration.
