Mathematical modeling and simulation of Mg-CO₂ primary battery for hydrogen generation and CO₂ utilization

Raman Kumar Singh, Prakash C. Ghosh and Venkatasailanathan Ramadesigan
Electrochimica Acta 531 (2025), 146333  ·  DOI: 10.1016/j.electacta.2025.146333
✉ Request Full Text

Abstract

After industrialization, atmospheric carbon dioxide (CO2) increased to 420 ppm, leading to critical environmental issues. Despite advances in carbon capture, utilization, and storage (CCUS) technologies, their economic feasibility and slow CO2 conversion rates hinder their effectiveness. Recently, metal-CO2 electrochemical systems that utilize the acidity from the spontaneous CO2 dissolution in aqueous solution have gained significant attention due to their potential for hydrogen and electrical energy generation. However, there is a lack of detailed transient analysis and mathematical models of these systems. A three-dimensional transient model is developed of a membrane-free Mg-CO2 primary battery for CO2 utilization, power generation, and hydrogen production by integrating Euler–Euler bubble flow, dilute solution theory, and electrochemical kinetics, and validated against existing literature data. It provides insight into real-time species concentration distribution, current and potential distribution, phase fractions, current density, volume-averaged species concentration, and electrochemical performance. Parametric analysis reveals that increasing electrolyte conductivity from 0.20 to 0.30 S m−1, reducing cathode porosity from 0.50 to 0.40, and shortening the anode–cathode gap from 3.5 cm to 2.5 cm significantly enhances power density by 37.64%, 2.46%, and 10.2% and hydrogen production over 24 h by 17.4%, 9.75%, and 31.3%, respectively. This work offers valuable insights for improving the performance of the membrane-free Mg-CO2 system, contributing to a more sustainable and environmentally friendly energy solution.

Keywords: CO2 utilization; Hydrogen production; Energy conversion; Mg-CO2 system modeling; Computational fluid dynamics

Highlights

  • Developed a three-dimensional physics-based isothermal model to predict real-time performance of the Mg-CO2 system, focusing on CO2 utilization and hydrogen production.
  • Coupled electrochemical, chemical absorption, and gas–liquid interaction phenomena in a membrane-free Mg-CO2 system.
  • Integrated Euler–Euler bubble flow, dilute solution theory, and electrochemical kinetics.
  • Investigated the effects of cathode porosity, electrolyte conductivity, and inter-electrode distance on system performance.
  • Analyzed CO2 bubble behavior, species transport, hydrogen production, current density, and power density.

Modeling Framework

The developed model is a three-dimensional, transient and isothermal multiphysics framework for a membrane-free Mg-CO2 primary battery. It couples gas–liquid bubble dynamics, CO2 absorption, ionic transport, electrochemical reactions, and hydrogen production.

Multiphase Flow
Euler–Euler bubble-flow modeling is used to describe CO2 gas transport and gas–liquid phase transformation.
Species Transport
Dilute solution theory and the Nernst–Planck equation describe diffusion, migration, and convection of ionic and non-ionic species.
Electrochemistry
Tertiary current distribution and concentration-dependent Butler–Volmer kinetics describe the electrochemical reactions.
CO₂ Conversion
CO2 absorption and chemical conversion generate H+ and HCO3−, enabling hydrogen production and Mg(HCO3)2 formation.

Model Validation

The developed model was validated against experimental polarization and power-density data reported by Kim et al. The simulated results closely matched the experimental data, with RMS errors of 1.50 mW cm−2 for power density and 2.20 mA cm−2 for current density.

Key Findings

Applied Potential
Hydrogen production increased from 0.76 to 5.60 L over 24 h when the potential decreased from 1.40 to 1.25 V.
Electrolyte Conductivity
Increasing conductivity from 0.20 to 0.30 S m−1 increased power density by 37.64% and hydrogen production by 17.4%.
Cathode Porosity
Reducing cathode porosity from 0.50 to 0.40 increased power density by 2.46% and hydrogen production by 9.75%.
Inter-electrode Distance
Reducing the distance from 3.5 cm to 2.5 cm increased maximum power density by 10.2% and hydrogen production by 31.3%.
