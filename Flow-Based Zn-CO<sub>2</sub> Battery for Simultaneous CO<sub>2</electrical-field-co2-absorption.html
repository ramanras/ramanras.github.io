Electrical Field Effect on CO2 Absorption and Chemisorption in a Rectangular Bubble Column

Raman Kumar Singh, Prakash Chandra Ghosh and Venkatasailanathan Ramadesigan
Department of Energy Science and Engineering, Indian Institute of Technology Bombay, Mumbai, India
COMSOL Conference · 2023  ·  COMSOL Paper ↗
📄 View COMSOL Paper ✉ Request Full Text

Abstract

The chemisorption and absorption of CO2 bubbles have been extensively studied and modeled in the literature. CO2 gas is converted into aqueous CO2, which subsequently produces OH−, HCO3−, and CO32− ions in the solution. This study presents a three-dimensional CO2 bubble-column model developed in COMSOL Multiphysics. Two cases are investigated: a bubble column without an applied electric field and a bubble column subjected to an electric field. The first model uses the Euler–Euler formulation together with the Bubble Flow and Dilute Solution Theory interfaces and is validated against existing literature. The second model extends the framework by accounting for electrostatic interactions between charged species and the applied electric field through the Nernst–Planck equation. The results demonstrate that the applied electric field modifies the distribution of charged species and their concentration gradients, thereby influencing the CO2 chemisorption process and mass-transfer behavior. A parametric study investigates mesh refinement, flow rate, and applied electric field.

Keywords: Bubble flow; Chemisorption; Dilute solution theory; Computational fluid dynamics; CO2 absorption

Research Highlights

  • Developed a three-dimensional transient multiphase model of a rectangular CO2 bubble column.
  • Modeled CO2 absorption and chemisorption using Euler–Euler bubble flow and dilute solution theory.
  • Incorporated the Nernst–Planck equation to account for electric-field-driven transport of charged species.
  • Investigated the effect of an applied electric field on the distribution of ionic species.
  • Studied the influence of mesh refinement, flow rate, and applied electric field on the model response.
  • Developed a modeling framework relevant to advanced CO2 capture and conversion systems.

Modeling Approach

The study uses a three-dimensional multiphase model to describe the interaction between CO2 gas bubbles and an aqueous electrolyte inside a rectangular bubble column. The model is divided into two stages. The first stage describes the bubble-column system without an electric field. The second stage extends the model by introducing the electrostatic effects associated with an applied electric field.

Euler–Euler Bubble Flow

The Euler–Euler formulation is used to describe the gas and liquid phases and their interaction within the rectangular bubble column.
CO₂ Absorption

Gas-phase CO2 is transferred from the bubbles into the aqueous phase and represented through the multiphase mass-transfer framework.
Dilute Solution Theory

Used to describe the transport and reaction of dissolved CO2 and ionic species in the electrolyte.
Nernst–Planck Equation

The electric-field model accounts for diffusion, convection, and electric-field-driven migration of charged species.

Species and Chemical Processes

The model considers the transformation of gaseous CO2 into dissolved CO2 followed by reactions involving hydroxide and carbonate species. The resulting aqueous system contains charged species including OH−, HCO3−, and CO32−. When an electric field is applied, the charged species experience electrostatic forces that modify their spatial distributions within the bubble column.

Effect of the Electric Field

The applied electric field changes the distribution of charged species within the electrolyte. Positively and negatively charged species migrate in opposite directions under the electric field, resulting in modified local concentration gradients. These changes influence the local chemical environment surrounding the dissolved CO2 and consequently affect the chemisorption behavior.

Without Electric Field

The charged species are transported through the conventional multiphase and species-transport mechanisms.
With Electric Field

Charged species additionally undergo migration caused by the applied electric potential.
Ion Redistribution

The electric field modifies the spatial concentration distribution of the ionic species.
CO₂ Chemisorption

Changes in local ionic concentrations influence the chemical conversion of dissolved CO2.

Model Validation

The model without an electric field was validated against existing literature data for the CO2 bubble-column system. The comparison showed agreement between the simulated concentration behavior and the reported literature results, providing a basis for extending the model to the electric-field case.

Parametric Study

  • Mesh refinement: investigated to establish an appropriate computational mesh for the three-dimensional bubble-column model.
  • Flow rate: investigated to understand its influence on gas–liquid transport and CO2 absorption behavior.
  • Applied electric field: investigated to determine its influence on ionic distributions and CO2 chemisorption.

Key Findings

  • The presence of an electric field significantly affects the CO2 chemisorption process in the rectangular bubble column.
  • The applied field changes the distribution of charged species within the aqueous phase.
  • Electric-field-driven migration modifies concentration gradients of the ionic species.
  • The resulting changes in concentration gradients influence the mass-transfer and chemisorption behavior of CO2.
  • The modeling framework provides a basis for investigating electric-field-assisted CO2 capture systems.
