Electrical Field Effect on CO2 Absorption and Chemisorption in a Rectangular Bubble Column

Raman Kumar Singh, Prakash Chandra Ghosh and Venkatasailanathan Ramadesigan
Department of Energy Science and Engineering, Indian Institute of Technology Bombay, Mumbai, India
✉ Request Full Text

Abstract

The chemisorption and absorption of CO2 bubbles have been extensively studied and modelled in the literature. CO2 gas is converted to aqueous CO2, which produces OH−, HCO3− and CO32− ions in the solution. In this study, a three-dimensional multiphase model of a CO2 bubble column is simulated with and without an electric field using COMSOL Multiphysics. The first model is developed using the Euler–Euler model and simulated using the bubble flow and dilute solution theory module in COMSOL, validated against data obtained from existing literature. The second model builds upon the first model by considering the electrostatic interaction between the charged species and the electrical field, employing the Nernst–Planck equation. Simulation results from the second model indicate that the presence of an electrical field significantly impacts the CO2 chemisorption process in the rectangular bubble column. The applied field alters the distribution of charged species, influencing concentration gradients and enhancing mass transfer rates. A parametric study is conducted on the applied electric field. The findings of this study can inform the design of novel CO2 capture systems, offering potential avenues for mitigating greenhouse gas emissions and advancing sustainable energy practices.

Keywords: Bubble flow; Chemisorption; Dilute solution theory; Computational fluid dynamics; CO2 absorption

Key Highlights

  • Developed a three-dimensional transient multiphase model of a rectangular CO2 bubble column.
  • Coupled Euler–Euler bubble flow with dilute solution theory for CO2 absorption and chemisorption.
  • Incorporated the Nernst–Planck equation to account for ionic migration under an applied electric field.
  • Investigated the concentration distributions of Na+, OH−, HCO3−, and CO32−.
  • Demonstrated the influence of electric-field-driven ion migration on CO2 chemisorption.

Modeling Approach

A three-dimensional transient Euler–Euler model is used to describe the gas and liquid phases in the rectangular bubble column. CO2 transfer from the gas phase to the liquid phase is coupled with dilute solution theory for species transport and chemical reactions. For the electric-field case, the transport of ionic species additionally accounts for migration caused by the applied electric potential through the Nernst–Planck equation.

The model considers aqueous CO2 together with Na+, OH−, HCO3− and CO32− species. The CO2 absorption and subsequent reactions lead to the formation of bicarbonate and carbonate species.

Simulation Setup

COMSOL Multiphysics 6.1 was used to simulate the transient model. The model utilizes bubbly-flow and laminar-flow physics for gas–liquid transport, tertiary electroneutrality for ionic species transport in the presence of an electric field, and transport of dilute species for the non-ionic species. The reported relative tolerance was 1 × 10−3.

Model Validation

The simulations were performed without an electric field for up to 30 seconds and validated against literature results for CO32− concentration. The reported comparison showed good agreement between the present model and the literature data. A mesh containing 437 domain elements was used for the study.

Effect of Electric Field

The applied electric field affects the movement of the charged species in the electrolyte. Positively charged species are attracted toward the negative pole, whereas negatively charged species migrate toward the positive pole. In the bubble column, the electric field therefore modifies the local concentration distributions of Na+, OH−, HCO3− and CO32−, influencing the local chemisorption behavior.

Results and Discussion

The volume-averaged gas-phase fraction increases rapidly initially and remains approximately constant for a period before increasing again. The reported gas-phase fraction does not show a significant change with the applied voltage because the same mass-transfer rate was used at the investigated voltages.

The concentration of OH− decreases with time, indicating its consumption during the formation of bicarbonate and carbonate species. The spatial distribution of carbonate ions changes substantially under an applied electric field, with negatively charged CO32− ions oriented toward the positive side of the bubble column.

Increasing the applied potential changes the concentration distributions of aqueous CO2 and carbonate species, demonstrating that electric-field-driven ionic migration can influence the CO2 conversion environment inside the bubble column.

Conclusion

A transient three-dimensional multiphase bubble-column model was developed using the Euler–Euler model, dilute solution theory, and the Nernst–Planck equation in the presence of an electric field. The model demonstrates that an applied electric field changes the distribution of ionic species and affects the concentration trends of CO2, HCO3−, OH− and CO32−. The modeling framework can be extended to investigate electrochemical devices in which CO2 is utilized in aqueous electrolytes, including metal–CO2 systems and electrochemical CO2 reduction.
